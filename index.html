About

I am a master's student in Electrical Engineering at the University of Pennsylvania, graduating in May 2027. I studied physics as an undergraduate at Jilin University.

My research so far has covered LLM agents and computer vision. My first-author paper diagnoses why LLM agents fail, using their execution trajectories, and checks whether each fix reaches the agent, changes its behavior, and improves the outcome. Before that, at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, I studied how to evaluate text-to-image generation.

Research interests

I am looking for a long-term direction where a physics background and machine learning experience reinforce each other. The questions I keep returning to are how models represent the rules of the world they act in, and how we can tell whether they have actually learned them.

  • LLM agentsDiagnosing failures from execution trajectories, and validating fixes beyond the final score.
  • Computer vision and generative modelsEvaluating what image and video models get right and wrong, including world models.
  • Physics and machine learningCombining physical models with data-driven methods, and asking how much physical structure a model needs.

Publications

Trace-to-Architecture: Capability Bottleneck Diagnosis for Harness Revision
Wenfeng Wang, Jianrui Shi, Mingang Guo, Jiayuan Ma, Yuqi Lu, Jiajun Sun, Guojiang Zhao, Jing Li
arXiv preprint arXiv:2610.16725, 2026
Under review · ACL Rolling ReviewALFWorldScienceWorldAppWorld

A framework that uses execution trajectories to decide what to change in an LLM agent's harness when it fails, and when those changes should apply. A development agent diagnoses failures with evidence from successful and failed tasks, revises the corresponding components, and checks that target tasks improve, successful tasks stay successful, and the edits are actually triggered. T2A achieves the highest aggregate score for all three evaluated task agents, exceeding their strongest baselines by 6.2 to 30.3 points.
