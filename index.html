About

I am a master's student in Electrical Engineering at the University of Pennsylvania, graduating in May 2027. I studied physics as an undergraduate at Jilin University.

My research focuses on LLM agents and NLP, with an emphasis on reasoning, verification, and evaluation. At Drexel University's Haz Lab, I am developing a benchmark for online formalization: how agents construct, reuse, and revise executable rules as natural-language requests evolve. My first-author paper, Trace-to-Architecture, diagnoses agent failures from execution trajectories and validates harness revisions. I have also studied speech recognition and LLM-generated clinical notes, and previously worked on text-to-image evaluation at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Research interests

I study how language-model agents reason about tasks, turn instructions into executable rules, and adapt as requirements change. I am particularly interested in evaluations that reveal why an agent succeeds or fails, and whether an improvement generalizes while preserving previously correct behavior.

  • LLM agents and adaptationDiagnosing capability bottlenecks from execution trajectories and guiding evidence-based harness revisions.
  • Online formalizationConstructing, reusing, and revising executable rule specifications across evolving request streams.
  • Reasoning, verification, and evaluationDesigning benchmarks and execution-based semantic evaluations for rule fidelity, generalization, and preservation of correct behavior.
  • NLP systemsUnderstanding how errors propagate through language and speech pipelines, including ASR and LLM-generated clinical notes.

Publications

Trace-to-Architecture: Capability Bottleneck Diagnosis for Harness Revision
Wenfeng Wang, Jianrui Shi, Mingang Guo, Jiayuan Ma, Yuqi Lu, Jiajun Sun, Guojiang Zhao, Jing Li
arXiv preprint arXiv:2610.16725, 2026
Under review · ACL Rolling ReviewALFWorldScienceWorldAppWorld

A framework that uses execution trajectories to decide what to change in an LLM agent's harness when it fails, and when those changes should apply. A development agent diagnoses failures with evidence from successful and failed tasks, revises the corresponding components, and checks that target tasks improve, successful tasks stay successful, and the edits are actually triggered. T2A achieves the highest aggregate score for all three evaluated task agents, exceeding their strongest baselines by 6.2 to 30.3 points.

Projects

Online Formalization of Evolving Natural-Language Requests
Haz Lab, Drexel University · with Prof. Li (Harry) Zhang · October 2026 – present
BenchmarkSemantic evaluationPython / CEL

I am developing a benchmark to investigate how LLM agents construct, reuse, and revise executable rule specifications over time. I designed multi-domain request streams with rule changes, exceptions, and recurring task patterns, together with execution-based semantic evaluations of rule fidelity, generalization, and preservation of previously established behavior. I implemented evaluation pipelines for Python and CEL specifications and conducted controlled experiments with Qwen3.5-9B and 27B to analyze formalization accuracy, program evolution, and computational cost.

Listening or writing? Where clinical note generation loses information
Independent project, 2026 · implemented with Claude Code
ASRLLM evaluationPriMock57

An evaluation of open-weight speech recognition and local LLM note writing on mock GP consultations, asking how much each stage costs the final note. In quiet audio, the choice of note-writing model moves fact recall by 8–12 points while ASR errors move it by 1–3; in 0 dB speech babble, the ASR's cost catches up. Word error rate does not predict note quality, and averages hide dangerous single errors such as a negated answer turning into its opposite.
